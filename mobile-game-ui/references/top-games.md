# What 151 top mobile games do

Sample: 151 games. The first pass (2026-11-26) covered 59: 49 from the US App Store top-grossing and
top-free game charts plus 10 long-running leaders in battler, roguelite and collector genres. The
second pass (2026-11-27) added 92 more from the top 100 of both charts. Notes combine each game's
store screens (three or four per game, viewed on those days) with its widely known in-game layout.
Store screens are partly marketing art and games change, so check the live game when one detail
matters. App icons of the top 150 were studied on one sheet: see [icons.md](icons.md).

Contents: tallies · puzzle and board · merge · coin looters and casino · 4X builders · arena and
card battlers · roguelite and idle RPG · hero collectors · shooters, sports, platforms · runners.

## Tallies across the sample

- **Orientation:** about three quarters portrait. Landscape is kept for shooters, sports, open-world
  RPGs, MOBAs and a few builders, where two thumbs steer.
- **Primary action:** in every game it is the largest warm-coloured control, bottom centre (portrait)
  or bottom right (landscape).
- **Currencies:** top edge in almost every game with a menu layer. Two or three currencies is normal
  for casual games, five or more for 4X.
- **Bottom navigation:** five tabs with the home tab in the middle and raised or highlighted is the
  most common portrait menu structure (puzzle, arena, roguelite, collector games).
- **Home background:** a live scene (base, room, board, hero) rather than a panel in the large
  majority.
- **Chrome weight:** newer hybrid-casual puzzle hits use almost no chrome: a thin top strip, the play
  area, one row of boosters. Older and midcore games carry more frames, ribbons and badges.
- **Type:** heavy rounded or condensed display faces with a dark outline, white or yellow fill.

## Second pass: 92 more games (charts of 2026-11-27)

Added from the US top-grossing 100 and top-free 100. One line each: what the screen is made of. The
families below matter more than any single game, because one template now serves dozens of hits.

### Family: tray-sort and "jam" puzzles (the 2026 hybrid-casual template)

Jelly Busters · Bus Fever Party · Bus Crowd · Jam Bus Sandfall · Yarn Loop · Food Hunt · Cube Smash ·
Cube Land · Screwdom · Sort Streak · Sand Blocks · Colorever · Triple Match 3D · Triple Match City ·
All in Hole · Hole Stars · Rotate Rings · Tidy Room

All share one screen, top to bottom:
1. A thin header: coins left, level centre, pause or gear right (three items, never more).
2. The **goal picture**: the thing being filled, built or cleared, often in a frame with a progress
   tag on its corner (3/5).
3. A **holding tray** of 5 to 7 empty slots. It is the fail state made visible: full tray, you lose.
4. The **supply field**: numbered or coloured pieces to tap.
5. Three or four round **boosters** on the bottom edge, each with a count badge or a green "+".

Why it works: the eye reads goal, then risk (tray), then choices, in the order it decides. No text is
needed. Backgrounds are one flat or softly graded colour so piece colours carry all the meaning.
Triple Match City goes furthest: the scene is black line art and only found items are coloured, so
colour itself is the reward.

### Family: lane and gate runners (crowd battlers)

Top Lords · Z Route · Puzzles & Survival (ad-style levels) · Lordrush · Top Force · Car Evolve ·
Sled Surfers · Just a Rock

The lane seen from behind fills the screen. Gates carry their effect as giant signed numbers
(+1, x2, -5) in blue for good and red for bad. Enemies and barrels wear their hit points as white
numbers. The HUD is an avatar and two pills at the top, or nothing at all. A boss gets one health
bar at the far end of the lane. Captions say one verb each. This is the nearest relative of a
castle lane battler: the world carries the numbers, the HUD stays out.

### Family: merge with story

Gossip Harbor · Merge Mansion · Seaside Escape · Hollywood Merge · Matching Story · Mystery Town ·
Hotel Legacy · Merge Cooking · Flambe · Foodstars

Board at the bottom, an **order strip** above it (customer portraits, the items they want, a coin
reward, a green Give button), pills on top (level star with bar, energy, coins, gems). The meta
layer is a place to restore, with locked areas marked by a sign and a price. Store shots sell the
drama, not the board: tilted photo piles, thought bubbles showing what the heroine needs, a diagonal
before/after split for renovation, and a choice card ("Help or ignore?").

### Family: slots and social casino

Lightning Link · Jackpot Party · Cashman · Quick Hit · Heart of Vegas · DoubleDown · Big Fish ·
Jackpot Magic · Monopoly Slots · Tang Luck · Bingo Blitz · Dice Dreams

One fixed bar at the bottom does all the input: bet with minus and plus, a centred SPIN (green or
gold, often a round lens), total win, MAX BET. Everything above is the machine, each with its own
logo art. Jackpot meters stack at the top in lit frames. Social casinos add a row of friends'
avatars at the very top. Wins are words: BIG WIN in a huge gradient with a counting number plate
and a small SKIP. Palette: purple or red with gold, the most saturated screens in the charts.

### What the second pass adds to the tallies

- **Chrome keeps shrinking.** In the free chart most hits show three header items and one row of
  boosters. If a number is not needed to decide the next tap, it is not on screen.
- **The fail state is a visible object** (the tray), not a hidden counter.
- **Numbers live in the world** in runner and lane games: on gates, on crowds, over bosses.
- **Mood sets the weight of the UI.** Calm puzzles are pale and nearly empty. Casino is loud gold.
  Romance and realistic shooters are dark and thin. Pick the weight from the mood first.
- **Legibility is sold as a feature** to older players: large tiles, high contrast, big type.
- **Store screenshots have their own grammar:** a coloured caption band at the top or bottom, two
  to four words in heavy outlined type, one verb per shot (Merge, Cook, Renovate), a mascot or a
  pointing hand showing the gesture.

## What to take from this for a new game

- Pick the row closest to the game, copy its **structure** (where things sit, how many tabs, what the
  one big button is), then invent the look.
- For a base-builder or lane battler in landscape, the closest structures are Clash of Clans (corners),
  Brawl Stars (lobby and results) and Clash Royale (cards with the resource under them).
- When two top games in a genre disagree, the newer chart entry usually has less chrome.
